## Petrodelphia: Environmental Justice & Civic Action 
Author: Angie Whistler
<br>

## Summary
<p>The neighborhoods of South and Southwest Philadelphia—including Grays Ferry, Point Breeze, and Eastwick—have long lived alongside one of the nation’s most powerful yet hazardous petrochemical corridors. For over 150 years, oil refineries, tank farms, and gas works generated energy and jobs—but with significant externalities: air and water pollution, fires and explosions, and elevated health risks. A catastrophic explosion in 2019 accelerated a civic reckoning.</p>

<p>Because refineries were among the few manufacturing employers that did not abandon Philadelphia during the post‑WWII period of deindustrialization and white flight, municipal and Commonwealth politicians often had incentives to preserve the status quo—protecting refinery jobs and revenues despite a long record of accidents and spills. This tension between economic security and public health is central to the unit’s inquiry. (See Farmer, 2025; Sasko, 2025; Varee, 2025.)</p>

<div id="purpose"></div>
### Purpose of These Materials
-  Build students’ historical and civic literacy around environmental justice (EJ) and fenceline communities in Philadelphia.
- Analyze the relationships among urban development, labor and deindustrialization, risk governance, and public health.
- Practice evidence‑based civic participation via hearings, policy briefs, map analysis, and data‑informed claims.


<div id="topics"></div>
### Topics Covered
- Environmental Justice 
- Civics & Government
- Race and Inequality
- Health and Pollution
- Urban Development
- 20th & 21st Century History
- Philadelphia


<div id="ideas"></div>
### Big Ideas
- Civics and U.S. History
- Environmental Health and Community Action


<div id="questions"></div>
### Essential Questions
- What responsibilities do governments and corporations have to protect community health?
- How can citizens influence environmental decisions in their neighborhoods?
- How do race and geography intersect in the history of pollution and environmental risk?
